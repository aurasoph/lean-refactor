## Proposed v4-A: leaderboard wrapper

Use `WRAPPER_ID = "v4-leaderboard-official-sum"`, `DEFAULT_EFFORT = "high"`, 18 distinct fast candidates, a 120-second fast-check timeout, two compatibility repairs, and a 30-minute episode limit. Those are search-budget controls, not alternative ranking criteria: candidates are always ranked solely by 100% survival and `objective_sum_pct`.

Prompt/tool changes:

- Retain v3’s official objective wording.
- Add a structural-search phase: consider changing the induction target, reusing an existing theorem that removes an induction, deleting dead proof blocks, and introducing small in-proof combinators.
- A failed implementation does not invalidate its proof strategy; retry promising structural ideas once with explicit constructor binders or a local helper.
- Documentation remains optional and targeted.
- No MCP or web. Permit editing, `rg`/`sed`, and the scorer. Direct `lake`/`lean` remains forbidden.

Exact replacement loop paragraph:

```text
Loop and stop rule (the runner enforces these limits). Maintain one automatic
incumbent among compiling candidates; higher `objective_sum_pct` is the only
comparison, and equal sums leave the existing incumbent unchanged. A fast check
counts only for a new proof hash; use at most 18. Do not stop merely because the
sum is positive. If the first compiling incumbent has `objective_sum_pct < 20`,
treat it only as a fallback: do not start the patience counter, and spend at
least six further checks on at least two genuinely different proof structures.
One of those branches must revisit the strongest failed structural idea using
explicit constructor binders or a small local helper before abandoning it. Once
the incumbent reaches 20, try at least four further distinct candidates and
stop only after four consecutive checks fail to improve it and at least ten
fast checks have been used. If the incumbent reaches 100, three further
candidates and three consecutive non-improvements after six total checks are
enough. At the check or wall-time limit, restore the incumbent and run one
all-version check. If survival is below 100%, make at most two new,
compatibility-directed repairs and check all versions once more; otherwise stop.
Never require both reduction components to be positive.
```

Concrete runner diff:

```diff
-WRAPPER_ID = "v3-official-objective"
-DEFAULT_EFFORT = "max"
+WRAPPER_ID = "v4-leaderboard-official-sum"
+DEFAULT_EFFORT = "high"
+FAST_CAP = 18
+COMPAT_CAP = 2
+FAST_TIMEOUT = 120
+EPISODE_TIMEOUT = 1800
```

Then:

- Start an episode-local scorer broker in the parent runner. `arena.py check` sends it the candidate hash/text; the trusted parent performs compilation.
- Archive every distinct candidate and result in `trials.jsonl`.
- Reject search checks after 18; cache unchanged hashes without spending a check.
- Maintain `best-fast.lean` by strict official sum and `best-robust.lean` by strict official sum plus 100% survival. Seed `best-robust` with the upstream reference.
- On exit or timeout, all-version-check the current and best-fast candidates if needed, then restore the highest-sum 100%-surviving checkpoint before writing `after.lean`.
- For official runs, seed later episodes from the global robust best. Retain `--fresh-reference` for wrapper evaluation and trace collection.
- Give Codex a restricted PATH with denial shims for `lake` and `lean`; the parent broker retains the real compiler environment. Codex supports repeatable inline config and explicit shell-environment settings in its [configuration reference](https://developers.openai.com/codex/config-reference) and [CLI reference](https://developers.openai.com/codex/cli/reference).

This spends more time than v3, deliberately: v1 shows that extra structured search buys substantial official score.

## Proposed v4-B: trace-harvest wrapper

Use a separate `WRAPPER_ID = "v4-trace-harvest-official-sum"` and always reset upstream. Cap at 12 fast checks and require three named proof families plus one consolidation attempt. Before each check, require a one-sentence hypothesis and a tag such as `structural`, `local`, `automation`, or `compat`. Archive candidate text, parent hash, hypothesis, errors, elapsed time, official sum, survival, and whether it became incumbent.

This produces cleaner preference/process data but is not the leaderboard wrapper: forced branch diversity and narration consume context and may knowingly test inferior candidates.
